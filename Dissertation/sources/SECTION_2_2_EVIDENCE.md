# Section 2.2 verified evidence packet

Scope: `2.2 Early-stage finance, portfolio reporting, and public company intelligence` only. Page
numbers are PDF page numbers in the hash-pinned local files. These claims were checked against
locally extracted text on 27 August 2026 and the expanded domain/landscape sources on 31 August 2026.

## Paragraph 1: information asymmetry and observable finance signals

- `estrin2024digital`, PDF pp. 1--5 and 12--13: equity-crowdfunding transactions are shaped by
  information asymmetry and platform/country heterogeneity; the analysed data focus on successful
  pitches, so findings do not describe all early-stage firms or unsuccessful campaigns.
- `wasti2024successive`, PDF pp. 1--3 and 6--12: successive crowdfunding rounds can operate as
  investor-facing quality signals in the studied Crowdcube setting, but the design is platform- and
  sample-specific and does not make the signal a universal measure of firm quality.

Safe synthesis: observable funding events can reduce information gaps and help structure portfolio
context, but they remain source-, platform- and sample-dependent signals rather than causal or
investment-quality labels.

## Paragraph 2: rounds, grants and public support as dated events

- `wasti2024successive`, PDF pp. 2--3 and 10--12: investors may interpret prior successful rounds as
  a signal, with effects contingent on the observed platform and campaign characteristics.
- `mondal2025state`, PDF pp. 1--2 and 7: the study links UKRI/Innovate UK participation to Companies
  House records and observes later performance differences, but explicitly states that causality is
  not proven and selection mechanisms may explain them.
- `thorne2026funding`, PDF pp. 1--6: reconstructing the UK funding lifecycle requires joining
  heterogeneous sources with no shared identifiers; coverage, historical data and conservative
  linkage remain incomplete.

Safe synthesis: funding rounds, grants and awards may be reported as attributed, dated events when
the relevant record supports them. They cannot by themselves establish future performance, causality
or complete funding history.

## Paragraph 3: legal identity and register intelligence

- `galanakis2026chrt`, PDF pp. 1--3 and 6: Companies House incorporation records can provide timely,
  broad coverage of registered-company creation, but legal incorporation is not economic activity;
  dormant firms, unincorporated businesses and self-employment limit interpretation.
- `hardman2023small`, PDF pp. 3, 5--6 and 21--25: Companies House snapshots support large-scale UK
  company analysis, but results are tied to a dated register state, corporate forms and filing
  definitions.
- `krasikov2020ready`, PDF pp. 5--9: corporate-register usefulness depends on the use case and on
  content quality, accessibility and interpretability rather than open availability alone.

Safe synthesis: a Companies House number is a defensible legal-identity key, but registry presence,
status or filing data should not be treated as a complete operational or financial profile.

## Paragraph 4: reporting and inference boundary

- `estrin2024digital`, PDF pp. 1--5 and 12--13, and `mondal2025state`, PDF p. 7: finance and support
  studies are context-specific and can be selected or confounded; observed association is not a
  general causal claim.
- `fca2024promotions`, PDF pp. 5, 9 and 13: financial communications can influence investment
  decisions and should present relevant benefits and risks in a fair, clear and balanced manner.
  This guidance supports a conservative reporting boundary, not a legal opinion about the artefact.

Safe synthesis: admissible portfolio intelligence states the event, legal entity, source, date,
locator and exact supported content, while making gaps and conflicts visible. It does not produce
automatic buy/sell advice, speculative valuation, causal performance attribution or a universal
company-quality score.

## Paragraph 5: sector scale and monitoring attention

- `britishbusinessbank2025equity`, PDF pp. 5 and 10: the report records \pounds10.8 billion of
  announced UK smaller-business equity investment across 2,048 deals in 2024, a 15.1 per cent fall
  in deal count, and weaker seed- and venture-stage deal activity.
- `gompers2016vcdecisions`, PDF pp. 1, 8, 28 and 34: the survey covers 885 institutional venture
  capitalists; respondents report an average of 18 hours per week working with portfolio companies
  and roughly 60 per cent interacting with them at least weekly.

Safe synthesis: the market is material and portfolio-company monitoring uses professional attention.
The figures are descriptive, drawn from different periods/settings, and do not measure portfolio
reporting effort or software benefit.

## Paragraph 6: private-market evidence limits

- `kaplan2016vcdata`, PDF pp. 3--7: venture-capital data are difficult to study because the market is
  private; commercial datasets differ in definitions and can contain omissions, backfill,
  non-reporting and under-coverage of failed firms. The working-paper and author-disclosure caveats
  remain explicit.
- `britishbusinessbank2025equity`, PDF pp. 55--57: the report treats UK angel-investment evidence as
  partial because much activity is private and reported data are patchy.

Safe synthesis: reporting should retain definitions, dates, legal identity and coverage gaps. Neither
source establishes automation effects on time, error, cost, satisfaction or decisions.

## Paragraphs 7--8: technical and commercial alternatives

- `buneman2001provenance`, PDF pp. 1--3, and `nikiforova2020quality`, PDF pp. 1--3 and 15--17:
  deterministic processing can be traceable only when origins, transformations and use-specific
  quality rules remain explicit.
- `gao2023ragsurvey`, PDF pp. 1--5 and 15--18, and `guo2024multiagent`, PDF pp. 3--5 and 10--11:
  retrieval and role decomposition introduce different coverage, coordination and evaluation risks;
  neither proves superiority over a simpler fixed process.
- `visible2026investors`, captured PDF pp. 1--4; `zapflow2026portfolio`, captured PDF pp. 1--4; and
  `affinity2026portfolio`, captured PDF pp. 1--3: official pages describe related portfolio-data,
  monitoring, relationship and reporting workflows.

Safe synthesis: compare the approaches against consistent workflow, evidence-control and unresolved-
evidence criteria. Vendor pages establish self-described capability only; unknown accuracy, reviewer
effort, time saving, UK early-stage suitability and comparative performance remain unknown.

## Proposed exhibit LIT-T2

Create a compact signal-to-inference boundary table with four evidence classes:

1. crowdfunding/funding round;
2. public grant or state-support record;
3. Companies House identity/filing event;
4. reconstructed funding-lifecycle link.

For each row show: what may be observed; the admissible report statement; the inference that remains
unsupported; and key literature sources. Label it as author synthesis, non-empirical and not a
scoring/ranking model. Include a complete text alternative and provenance. Do not include corpus
counts, result metrics or recommendations.

## Prohibited overreach

- Do not call funding, award, filing or incorporation events proof of firm quality, innovation impact,
  financial health or future success.
- Do not generalise crowdfunding results beyond their samples/platforms/countries.
- Do not imply the public funding or company-register record is complete or current for every use.
- Do not make legal, investment, causal or valuation recommendations.
- Do not introduce this artefact's empirical results.
