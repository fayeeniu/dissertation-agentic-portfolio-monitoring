# Section 1.1 verified evidence packet

Scope: `1.1 Early-stage portfolio reporting and company intelligence` only. Page numbers below are
PDF page numbers in the hash-pinned files under `sources/papers`; printed page numbers are included
where the journal pagination differs. These claims were checked against locally extracted text on
27 August 2026.

## Paragraph 1: public evidence is useful but structurally incomplete

- `hardman2023small`, PDF pp. 21--25 (printed pp. 137--141): the whole-register study reports that
  99.87% of its limited-company comparison set was private; its account-type table includes large
  `no accounts filed` and empty-field groups; newer companies are less likely to have account
  information; and recent public companies can show `no detail available` because of filing lag.
- `galanakis2026chrt`, PDF pp. 2 and 5--6: Companies House provides timely, broad legal-registration
  data, but legal incorporation is not the same as economic activity; live records can include
  dormant firms and exclude unincorporated/self-employed businesses.

Safe synthesis: official public records give a necessary legal and temporal starting point for UK
company intelligence, but they cannot be treated as a complete operational or financial picture of
an early-stage company.

## Paragraph 2: investment-relevant evidence is event-based and distributed

- `estrin2024digital`, PDF pp. 6--7 and 12--13: crowdfunding outcomes depend on pitch, platform,
  country, sector, region and period context; the study covers successful pitches and does not make
  investor count a causal measure of venture quality.
- `wasti2024successive`, PDF pp. 6--12: the unit of analysis is a campaign round; the released UK
  Crowdcube study reconstructs repeated rounds and finds associations with investor count, target,
  equity offered and overfunding, while acknowledging its single-platform limit.
- `thorne2026funding`, PDF pp. 1--6: the UK funding lifecycle spans opportunities, applications,
  decisions, projects, organisations and outcomes; links and extracted fields have incomplete
  coverage and errors include plausible values absent from source text.

Safe synthesis: a useful portfolio view must join dated, source-specific events rather than reduce
company evidence to one score; the events remain observations, not automatic investment conclusions.

## Paragraph 3: auditability is a data and evidence problem before it is a prose problem

- `nikiforova2020quality`, PDF pp. 15--17 (printed pp. 121--123): the Companies House worked example
  found use-case-specific defects, including empty mandatory fields, and advocates a readable quality
  specification linked to executable validation and a retained test protocol.
- `krasikov2020ready`, PDF pp. 5--9: source usability is relative to intended use; legal identity and
  address fields are more available than organisational, management, financial and contact data;
  the proposed assessment covers use context, source identification, metadata and content.
- `bradley2024synfintabs`, PDF pp. 4 and 6--8: financial-table extraction can fail through OCR,
  repeated values, wrong locations or lost negative signs; exact cell/word position and end-to-end
  evaluation are therefore material to evidence fidelity.

Safe synthesis: fluent generation is downstream of identity, source fitness, temporal meaning,
extraction, and traceability. A defensible report must retain the route from each material statement
to an admitted source and disclose absent or ambiguous evidence.

## Prohibited overreach

- Do not equate legal incorporation with operating activity.
- Do not describe financing events, repeat rounds or investor counts as causal quality signals.
- Do not infer live-project effectiveness, investment performance or user benefit from these papers.
- Do not cite an inaccessible page, a search snippet, or the project literature-review notes as the
  underlying source; citations must resolve to the PDFs listed above.
