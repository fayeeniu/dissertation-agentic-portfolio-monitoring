DISC-T3: Literature and result compatibility check

Purpose: compare the reported evidence with the admitted literature without treating qualitative
agreement as a statistical anomaly test.

RQ1
Literature expectation: traceability, typed data-quality rules and reproducibility controls make
decisions inspectable but do not establish source truth or field accuracy.
Observed: identity, time, provenance, verification and export controls executed; fourteen D0 cases
repeated exactly.
Judgement: directionally compatible controlled mechanism feasibility; no field-accuracy estimate.

RQ2
Literature expectation: claim correctness, citation support and attribution are separate; a support
gate should withhold represented unsupported material.
Observed: C2 removed six D0 false positives, retained all five supported cases and changed precision
from 0.455 to 1.000 without D0 recall loss.
Judgement: mechanistically compatible, but the perfect values are construction-aligned and do not
establish a normal population effect.

RQ3
Literature expectation: human control may improve reflection and accountability while creating
reliance, preference, workload and delay trade-offs.
Observed: named review controls exist, but C0 and C3 each have zero observations.
Judgement: neither supportive nor anomalous; RQ3 is unanswered.

Generalisation
Literature expectation: independent holdout evidence, leakage control, complete accounting and
explicit uncertainty are required before external claims.
Observed: D0 labels and rules were co-developed; D1 is protocol-only, D2 is sealed, and live/company
comparisons are unrun.
Judgement: this is an expected evidence gap, not a statistical anomaly or non-anomaly finding.

Sources: Gao et al. (2023a, 2023b), Pineau et al. (2021), Nikiforova et al. (2020), Gebru et al.
(2021), Kapoor and Narayanan (2023), Amershi et al. (2019) and Buçinca et al. (2021).
