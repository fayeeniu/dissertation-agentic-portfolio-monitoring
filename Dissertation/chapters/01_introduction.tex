\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Early-stage portfolio reporting and company intelligence}

Early-stage portfolio reporting first asks which legal entity is described, and at what date.
Companies House provides a broad, timely baseline for incorporated firms, but incorporation is not
economic activity; live records may include dormant companies and exclude unincorporated businesses
and self-employed people \citep{galanakis2026chrt}. The register is also disclosure-lagged: many
companies have no accounts filed, while recent records may lack detail because filing follows
incorporation \citep{hardman2023small}. Stable identifiers and explicit as-of dates are therefore
interpretive controls, not administrative decoration.

Evidence about company development is distributed across dated events, not held in a single profile.
Crowdfunding observations vary by pitch, platform, country, sector, region and period, while released
samples may cover successful pitches only \citep{estrin2024digital}. Repeat-funding research treats
campaign round as its unit and reports platform-specific associations with investor participation,
targets, equity offered and overfunding \citep{wasti2024successive}. UK funding-data reconstruction
connects opportunities, applications, decisions, organisations, projects and outcomes, yet records
incomplete coverage and uncertain links \citep{thorne2026funding}. Reporting should therefore
preserve event and source boundaries, not convert observations into a causal company-quality score.

These limitations make auditability a data problem before fluent synthesis. Corporate-register
fitness depends on intended use: legal identity and address fields are more available than management,
financial or contact information \citep{krasikov2020ready}; even mandatory Companies House fields can
be empty, motivating readable, use-case-specific quality requirements linked to executable validation
\citep{nikiforova2020quality}. Financial-table extraction adds OCR, repeated-value, cell-location and
negative-sign errors \citep{bradley2024synfintabs}. Evidence capture must retain source identity,
observation time, extraction location and validation status, allowing material statements to be traced
and absent or ambiguous evidence to be disclosed.

\section{Problem definition and motivation}

The problem is to identify the legal entity represented by heterogeneous records and interpret each
field at its availability time. Cross-register research used suffix removal, country-alias
normalisation, fuzzy matching and manual review, yet left unmatched cases
\citep{surak2026gateways}. Companies House data can contain empty mandatory fields and inconsistent
country labels \citep{nikiforova2020quality}, while recent firms may show no accounts or no detail
because filing occurs later \citep{hardman2023small}. The local design holds records until company
number and legal identity are resolved, preserves missing states rather than turning blanks into
zeros, and records availability time to avoid projecting later knowledge backwards.

Fluent synthesis can remain unsafe despite citations. Evaluating cited generation distinguishes
answer correctness, citation recall and citation precision; none is guaranteed by a marker alone
\citep{gao2023alce}. Attribution does not establish truth if its source is wrong
\citep{gao2023rarr}. Retrieved public text can be interpreted as instructions, enabling indirect
prompt injection that manipulates summaries, source selection or tool actions
\citep{greshake2023indirect}. The local contract therefore bounds web search to candidate discovery,
not exhaustive coverage. A report claim requires admitted immutable evidence where capture is
permitted, an exact locator and separate verification; retrieved text remains untrusted.

Human control is necessary but not automatically effective. General human--AI guidance recommends
stating capability and uncertainty, allowing correction or dismissal, explaining behaviour and
providing global controls \citep{amershi2019guidelines}. In a controlled task, participants sometimes
over-relied on incorrect AI suggestions; forcing reflection reduced over-reliance but created
preference, trust and subgroup trade-offs \citep{bucinca2021forcing}. The project response is to expose
conflicts, missing evidence, limitations and exact locators, then require named approval before export.
These are safeguards, not evidence of better decisions, reduced manual effort or time savings. Their
benefit remains a question for an authorised human-in-the-loop study; no production or live-use claim
follows. Figure~\ref{fig:problem-controls-rqs} presents a non-empirical conceptual mapping from these
risks, through the local controls, to RQ1--RQ3.

\section{Aim and research questions}

Following design science, this dissertation aims to build and evaluate a bounded multi-agent artefact
for portfolio ingestion, validation and report generation. The primary question asks to what extent it
can automate the end-to-end workflow and how quality and reliability compare with manually produced
reports. Construction alone does not establish utility; evaluation against stated objectives is
required \citep{hevner2004design}. The process links problem definition to development, demonstration
and measurement \citep{peffers2007dsrm}. Table~\ref{tab:research-contract} records the questions as a
non-result research contract.

RQ1 asks how accurately and reliably the bounded workflow transforms heterogeneous portfolio inputs
into typed, temporally eligible, provenance-complete and reviewable claims without collapsing
uncertainty. Measures separate answer correctness, citation support and provenance completeness
\citep{gao2023alce}. RQ2 asks, under the same information boundary, how bounded independent
verification changes supported-claim precision, recall, unsupported-claim rate, contradiction
handling, abstention, provenance completeness and repeat consistency against a deterministic path
without independent verification; attribution alone cannot establish correctness
\citep{gao2023rarr}. C0 is manual, C1 deterministic without an independent verifier, C2 bounded
independent verification, and C3 C2 plus named review.

RQ3 asks, where separately authorised, how automated and named-review conditions compare with the
manual workflow across report correctness, completeness, active and elapsed time, edit burden,
approval time and reviewer utility. Human--AI guidance supports exposing capability, uncertainty,
correction and controls \citep{amershi2019guidelines}, while forcing interventions can create
preference, trust and subgroup trade-offs \citep{bucinca2021forcing}. C0/C3 human evidence remains
NO-GO and held pending authority; without authority at freeze, RQ3 remains unanswered and the primary
question only partly answered. Public-web company research remains an implemented engineering case
study outside these questions.

\section{Research objectives}

Table~\ref{tab:research-contract} defines the question and evidence contract. O1 specifies a bounded
data and evidence model covering typed claims, legal identity, availability time, explicit missing
states, source admission, exact locators and provenance; O2 implements it as a fail-closed local
workflow with independent-verification and named-approval boundaries. Together, O1 and O2 primarily
operationalise RQ1 and the primary question. O3 tests RQ2 through the frozen C1/C2 comparison under one
information boundary, using fidelity, support, contradiction, abstention, provenance completeness and
repeat consistency. These prospective objectives join an artefact to explicit evaluation criteria
\citep{hevner2004design} and move from problem-derived objectives through development, demonstration
and evaluation \citep{peffers2007dsrm}; they do not establish utility.

O4 tests RQ3 only under separate authority by evaluating C0--C3 manual and human-control outcomes for
correctness, completeness, time, edits, approval and reviewer utility. It contributes to the primary
question only if authorised. Systems should expose capability, uncertainty, correction and controls
\citep{amershi2019guidelines}, but human interventions can create preference, trust and subgroup
trade-offs \citep{bucinca2021forcing}. O5 applies across the primary question and RQ1--RQ3 as a
reporting discipline: distinguish supported, unsupported, contradictory, unavailable, null and held
evidence, and keep answer correctness separate from citation support and attribution
\citep{gao2023alce,gao2023rarr}. C0/C3 evidence remains held without authority and must not be replaced
by D0.

\section{Scope, contribution, and exclusions}

The artefact is scoped as a local, loopback-only, single-user research prototype, not a deployed
service. Its intended purpose, user, context and operational boundary are stated explicitly because
risk management depends on a defined use context and a separate deployment decision
\citep{nist2023airmf}. Public company-level information may cross the external-model boundary only
through admitted controls. Restricted or internal material remains local and is excluded from public
generative-AI applications or APIs \citep{cddo2023genai}. This boundary does not establish legal
compliance, production security or provider-side zero retention.

The contribution is bounded to an evidence-first artefact, a reproducible evaluation protocol and a
traceable reporting method. This combines design and evaluation against declared objectives without
treating implementation as demonstrated utility \citep{hevner2004design,peffers2007dsrm}. The method
keeps answer correctness separate from citation support and attribution, so sourced claims still
require passage-level verification \citep{gao2023alce,gao2023rarr}. The implemented public-web
company-research path is included only as a live-unrun engineering case study. It supplies
architecture and adversarial-control evidence, not live company findings, exhaustive coverage,
operational benefit or general superiority.

The scope excludes buy/sell recommendations, speculative valuations, person profiling, autonomous
publication, and claims of remote, multi-tenant or production readiness. Financial communications
may affect investment decisions and can create additional duties, supporting a conservative boundary
rather than a claim of legal compliance \citep{fca2024promotions}. Retrieved public content also
remains untrusted because indirect prompt injection can manipulate model outputs or tool actions
\citep{greshake2023indirect}. Named approval is required before export, consistent with accountable
human responsibility and oversight \citep{cddo2023genai}. These controls are project safeguards; they
do not prove universal prevention, improved decisions or human benefit.

\section{Dissertation roadmap}

Chapter 2 will critically review the literature and define the research gap. Chapter 3 will specify
the frozen methodology, C0--C3 conditions, evidence tiers and authorisation-dependent holds. Chapter 4
will describe the implemented artefact and distinguish the live-unrun company-research case study from
evaluated evidence. Chapter 5 will report engineering and synthetic results while marking unavailable
outcomes explicitly. Chapter 6 will interpret those findings proportionately against RQ1--RQ3 and the
primary question, without treating missing evidence as success. Chapter 7 will examine governance,
ethics, security and validity limitations. Chapter 8 will draw bounded conclusions and sequence the
future evidence needed to answer held questions. This progression follows design science by connecting
a problem, artefact, rigorous evaluation and communication \citep{hevner2004design}; it also reflects
movement from problem identification and objectives through development, demonstration and evaluation,
with iteration where evidence requires it \citep{peffers2007dsrm}. The sequence is prospective, not
completed chapters or predetermined results.
